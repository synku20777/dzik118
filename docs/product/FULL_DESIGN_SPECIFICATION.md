# Riga Architectural Spectrum — Brand Identity and Digital UI Design Specification

iturn0image4

Riga is unusually well suited to a layered digital identity because its historic center is not defined by one architectural period. UNESCO explicitly recognizes the combination of medieval urban fabric, Art Nouveau/Jugendstil, and wooden architecture as central to Riga's outstanding heritage value, while Riga's contemporary cultural buildings add a newer layer rather than replacing the older ones. citeturn1search0turn4view3turn2search0

The strongest design direction is therefore **not “make the interface look Art Nouveau.”** It is:

> **Build the product the way Riga is built: rigorous structure underneath, craftsmanship at human scale, selective ornament at moments of emphasis, and contemporary clarity over the whole system.**

For Property Billing specifically, this is useful because the product needs to feel trustworthy, civic, durable, local, and financially serious—not luxurious, folkloric, or touristic.

## Brand foundation

### Core narrative: Riga, built in layers

The brand should express Riga as a city whose identity comes from **successive architectural layers coexisting**. Medieval streets establish structure; Jugendstil façades introduce controlled individuality; wooden neighborhoods contribute warmth and craft; contemporary public architecture introduces restraint, openness, and functional clarity. UNESCO's description of Riga and Latvia's official architectural sources support exactly this coexistence rather than a single-style reading of the city. citeturn1search0turn4view3turn8search3

A good verbal foundation would be:

> **Built for everyday life. Shaped by Riga.**
>
> Property administration is infrastructural: repetitive, important, and expected to endure. The visual system takes its cues from Riga's architecture—precise enough to support complex work, warm enough to feel residential, and distinctive without decoration becoming the product.

An alternative, more institutional version:

> **Order, craft, continuity.**
>
> A digital system grounded in the material and architectural character of Riga.

### The aesthetic ratio

I would not give every architectural layer equal visual weight. Doing so would create a historical collage.

Use approximately:

| Influence | Role in the digital system |
|---|---|
| **Contemporary Baltic restraint — 60–70%** | Spacing, layout, typography, data density, functional clarity |
| **Hanseatic / medieval structure — 15–20%** | Grid, vertical hierarchy, dividers, strong structural alignment |
| **Wooden Riga — 10–15%** | Warm neutrals, tactility, approachable residential character |
| **Jugendstil / National Romanticism — 5–10%** | Identity moments, curves, icon details, display typography, ornamental separators |

That hierarchy is a design recommendation rather than a claim about the historical proportions of Riga itself. It deliberately keeps the product operational while drawing on the city's recognized mix of medieval, Art Nouveau, and wooden architecture. citeturn1search0turn8search3turn4view3

### Architectural layer to interface principle

**Jugendstil / Art Nouveau should mean “controlled expression,” not decoration everywhere.** Riga's Alberta Street façades are characterized by figurative sculpture, balconies, columns, abstract curves, and strongly composed façades; official Riga material identifies Alberta Street as a particularly concentrated architectural ensemble. citeturn8search8turn8search6

Translate that into:

- bespoke display-letter details;
- organic but disciplined curves;
- occasional asymmetry;
- delicate line ornaments;
- distinctive icons;
- decorative treatment concentrated at headings, empty states, marketing surfaces, and significant workflow milestones.

Do **not** put floral motifs behind invoices or use decorative borders around every card.

**Medieval / Hanseatic Riga should supply the structural skeleton.** Riga's Old Town retains medieval urban fabric and contains Gothic and later architectural layers, while Riga Cathedral itself combines Romanesque, early Gothic, Baroque, and Art Nouveau elements. citeturn1search0turn8search3turn1search13

Translate that into:

- vertical rhythm;
- strong columns;
- narrow structural dividers;
- sharply organized information;
- tall, confident page headings;
- stable left-navigation geometry;
- clear “foundation → level → detail” hierarchy.

**Wooden Riga should contribute humanity and material warmth.** Latvia's official description identifies Pārdaugava as a center of Riga's wooden heritage and specifically describes Ķīpsala as a setting where restored timber buildings coexist with modern structures. citeturn4view3

Translate that into:

- warm limestone/off-white rather than sterile blue-white;
- muted pine and brown undertones;
- fine panel-like separators;
- restrained tactile textures in brand surfaces;
- slightly softer residential sections;
- generous but not extravagant spacing.

**Contemporary Riga should govern usability.** The National Library of Latvia, designed by Gunārs Birkerts and occupied since 2014, is a particularly clear example of contemporary cultural architecture carrying symbolic references while functioning as a modern public building. citeturn2search0turn2search20

Translate that into:

- large fields of negative space;
- legible data tables;
- direct controls;
- minimal elevation;
- functional responsive behavior;
- ornament that never interferes with task completion.

### What the identity should explicitly avoid

Do not make the product look like a Riga tourism site.

Avoid:

**literal skyline silhouettes**, St. Peter's spires as repeated decoration, cats, cobblestone textures, postcard photographs behind dashboards, folk-pattern borders around every element, fake parchment, ornate gold frames, script fonts, or faux-19th-century interfaces.

Riga's actual architectural identity is more sophisticated than those symbols: official heritage descriptions emphasize the coexistence of medieval structure, Art Nouveau, wooden neighborhoods, and later urban layers. citeturn1search0turn4view3

## Mood board and visual direction

### Direction: Quiet Centre precision

This should be the dominant brand expression.

Take from Alberta and Elizabetes streets:

**pale mineral façades + oxidized metal + dark wrought iron + highly ordered vertical windows + occasional sculptural surprise.**

Riga tourism and the Art Nouveau museum identify the Quiet Centre and Alberta Street as key concentrations of elaborate Art Nouveau architecture, with façades combining strong composition and ornament. citeturn1search2turn8search8

Digital translation:

```text
Pale limestone canvas
        ↓
Dark structural typography
        ↓
Daugava slate primary
        ↓
Verdigris accents
        ↓
One ornamental detail per composition
```

A dashboard should feel like an orderly façade from a distance. Only when the user gets closer should details emerge.

### Direction: Old Riga vertical structure

Use Old Town less as a texture reference and more as a **composition reference**.

The medieval core contains narrow streets and architectural layers that include Gothic buildings and prominent vertical religious structures. citeturn8search3turn1search0

Translate this into:

- 12-column desktop grid;
- strong vertical table alignment;
- tall section headings;
- 1px “masonry joint” dividers;
- left-edge state indicators;
- compressed horizontal controls when space is tight;
- clear top-to-bottom hierarchy.

A useful motif is the **gable** reduced to geometry:

```text
   /\
  /  \
 |    |
 |    |
```

Do not literally draw houses everywhere. The useful idea is **rise → apex → resolution**.

That can subtly influence:

- chart peaks;
- accordion indicators;
- selected navigation markers;
- logo construction;
- illustration framing.

### Direction: Ķīpsala craft

Ķīpsala and Pārdaugava should inform the product's residential side rather than its administrative skeleton. Latvia's official architecture material describes Pārdaugava's long wooden-building tradition and Ķīpsala's combination of restored timber houses with contemporary structures. citeturn4view3

Translate:

```text
warm cream
pine-grey
charcoal
subtle horizontal panel rhythm
small-radius corners
soft tactile surfaces
```

This is especially appropriate for:

- resident dashboard;
- dwelling profile;
- empty states;
- onboarding;
- communications;
- account summary cards.

### Direction: Daugava contemporary

Treat “Baltic modernism” here as a **design stance**, rather than claiming a single formally codified Riga architectural school.

The contemporary layer should feel:

- spacious;
- cool;
- direct;
- civic;
- quiet;
- materially honest.

The National Library provides a useful precedent: a contemporary building can carry strong Latvian symbolism without becoming historical imitation. citeturn2search0turn2search20

That is precisely the right goal for the digital product.

### Accessibility validation

WCAG 2.1 requires at least **4.5:1 for normal text at AA**, **7:1 for normal text at AAA**, with 3:1 allowed for qualifying large text at AA. Required graphical and UI component boundaries/states need at least 3:1 against adjacent colors. citeturn0search7turn0search18

Using the WCAG luminance/contrast method, the proposed core combinations calculate approximately as follows:

| Foreground | Background | Ratio | Normal-text result |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `#17242B` | `#F7F4EE` | **14.45:1** | AAA |
| `#58666C` | `#F7F4EE` | **5.42:1** | AA |
| `#FFFCF7` | `#274B5B` | **9.15:1** | AAA |
| `#FFFCF7` | `#35677A` | **6.08:1** | AA |
| `#FFFCF7` | `#2F736D` | **5.41:1** | AA |
| `#FFFCF7` | `#9A493A` | **6.06:1** | AA |
| `#FFFCF7` | `#7C470F` | **7.42:1** | AAA |
| `#FFFCF7` | `#4F5D3A` | **6.93:1** | AA, just below AAA |
| `#F2EEE6` | `#0E1517` | **15.94:1** | AAA |
| `#B7C0BD` | `#0E1517` | **9.92:1** | AAA |

This means `amber-500 #C17B26` should **not** be used as a white-text button simply because it looks like a warning color. Use dark `amber-700` for text/button fills and reserve the brighter amber for charts, markers, or large graphical accents.

### Semantic architecture

Avoid the common mistake of turning historical material colors directly into semantic meanings.

Instead:

```text
Daugava = action / navigation
Verdigris = success / resolved
Amber = attention / warning
Brick = error / destructive
Pine = positive secondary state
Limestone = structure / neutral surfaces
```

That gives the historical palette a coherent operational role.

## Typography system

Typography is where the architectural reference can become distinctive without making the workflow harder to use.

### Recommended production pairing

**UI and body: Source Sans 3**

Google Fonts describes Source Sans 3's predecessor as Adobe's first open-source type family and specifically notes that it was intended to work well in user interfaces. citeturn6search0

Use it for:

- navigation;
- tables;
- forms;
- invoices;
- numbers;
- buttons;
- descriptions;
- resident UI;
- dense administration.

Recommended system:

```text
Caption        12 / 16   500
Label          13 / 18   600
Body small     14 / 20   400
Body           16 / 24   400
UI strong      16 / 22   600
Heading 3      20 / 26   600
Heading 2      28 / 34   600
```

Do not replace functional UI typography with an ornate period face.

### Display: Fraunces

Fraunces is explicitly described by Google Fonts as an Old Style soft-serif display family influenced by early twentieth-century typefaces. That period connection makes it much more appropriate than a generic “vintage” script for selectively echoing the Jugendstil/National Romantic era. citeturn6search1

Use Fraunces for:

- product wordmark;
- major period/month headings;
- marketing pages;
- annual/financial reports;
- selected dwelling numbers;
- empty-state titles.

Example:

```text
October 2026
Fraunces 40 / 44
weight 550–600

Billing workflow
Source Sans 3 18 / 24
weight 650
```

Do not use Fraunces inside:

- table cells;
- form fields;
- buttons;
- badges;
- fine print.

### Better long-term solution: custom display lettering

The most ownable identity would be:

> **Source Sans 3 + bespoke Riga display alphabet**

Do not design an entire custom body font initially.

Commission a limited set of:

- A–Z;
- Latvian diacritics;
- numerals;
- punctuation;
- product wordmark;
- large headings.

Its construction can borrow from:

- steep National Romantic roof geometry;
- elongated Jugendstil proportions;
- rounded Art Nouveau terminals;
- carved-stone verticals.

That gives the identity something competitors cannot obtain by selecting the same Google Font.

### Latvian language is non-negotiable

Before locking either display or UI fonts, test real Latvian strings:

```text

Ūdens patēriņš
Rēķina saņēmējs
Brīvības iela
Ķīpsala
Āgenskalns
Parāds

```

Do not approve a brand font by looking only at English specimen text.

## UI components and spatial system

The most important principle is:

> **Architectural reference belongs in construction, not decoration.**

### Grid and spacing

Use an 8px base system:

```text
4   micro
8   compact
12  control internal
16  standard
24  component
32  section
48  large section
64  page rhythm
96  brand/marketing
```

Desktop administration:

```text
Sidebar     240–256px
Main max    ~1440px
Grid        12 columns
Gutter      24px
Page pad    32–48px
```

The medieval/Gothic influence appears through strong vertical alignment rather than medieval ornament. Riga's historic core provides the structural reference; modern spacing prevents that structure from becoming oppressive. citeturn1search0turn8search3

### Cards: “cut limestone”

Default:

```css
background: #FFFCF7;
border: 1px solid #D5CEC2;
border-radius: 10px;
box-shadow: none;
```

Avoid generic SaaS floating cards everywhere.

Most components should feel **set into the composition**, like blocks in a façade.

Elevation should be exceptional.

### Elevation tokens: layered stonework

```css
--elevation-0: none;

--elevation-1:
  0 1px 2px rgb(23 36 43 / 0.05),
  0 4px 12px rgb(23 36 43 / 0.04);

--elevation-2:
  0 2px 4px rgb(23 36 43 / 0.06),
  0 12px 28px rgb(23 36 43 / 0.08);

--elevation-overlay:
  0 8px 20px rgb(23 36 43 / 0.10),
  0 30px 80px rgb(23 36 43 / 0.14);
```

Use:

- elevation 0: tables, normal cards;
- elevation 1: menus, sticky toolbars;
- elevation 2: raised contextual elements;
- overlay: drawer/modal only.

Dark mode should rely more on borders and surface differentiation than shadow.

### Buttons

Primary:

```text
Daugava slate fill
min 40px height
8px radius
600 weight
```

Do not make the primary button completely pill-shaped. A modest radius feels closer to carved/constructed forms and more appropriate for an administrative product.

Secondary:

```text
surface background
stone border
dark text
```

Tertiary:

```text
text only
underline/arrow on hover where relevant
```

Destructive:

```text
brick red
```

Amber is warning, not destructive.

### Inputs

Current generic rectangular inputs can become materially stronger with very little change:

```css
height: 40px;
border: 1px solid #8F887E;
border-radius: 7px;
background: #FFFCF7;
```

Focus:

```css
border-color: #274B5B;
box-shadow: 0 0 0 3px rgb(39 75 91 / 0.14);
```

The label should remain outside the field.

Avoid placeholder-as-label patterns.

### Tables

Tables are where the Gothic/modern structural idea is most useful.

Use:

- strong column alignment;
- minimal zebra striping;
- thin stone borders;
- state colors as small accents;
- tabular numerals;
- left-edge exception bars;
- generous enough 52–60px rows.

Do not flood entire rows red/green/blue.

A 2px status “structural line” at the left works particularly well:

```text
│ Missing data
│
│ Dwelling 7
│ Cold water missing
```

It echoes vertical architectural rhythm without becoming illustrative.

### Status chips

Reduce rounded SaaS-pill styling.

Preferred:

```text
radius: 5–6px
padding: 3px 7px
small icon + text
```

For example:

```text
! Missing data
✓ Prepared
↗ Sent
● Paid
```

Never rely on color alone, consistent with WCAG's requirements around distinguishability and non-text state communication. citeturn0search18turn0search23

### Drawers

Your newly adopted context-preserving drawer pattern is especially compatible with this system.

Treat the drawer like **a contemporary extension attached to an older structural façade**:

```text
PAGE                        DRAWER
│                           │
│ workbench                 │ Context
│ remains visible           │ DWELLING 3
│                           │
│                           │ Billing details
│                           │ Draft
│                           │
│                           │ ─────────────
│                           │ form
│                           │
│                           │ blockers
│                           │
│                           │ ─────────────
│                           │ Cancel  Save
```

Recommended drawer changes:

- width 480–520px on large desktop;
- 28–32px horizontal padding;
- sticky header;
- scrollable content;
- sticky footer;
- actual form labels above fields rather than squeezed beside them;
- related fields grouped into sections;
- blockers explicitly shown near the relevant fields;
- contextual “Open full dwelling” as a low-emphasis secondary link.

This preserves the “modern functional shell” while the field separators and fine strokes provide the architectural character.

### Dwelling detail page

The dwelling page should become hierarchical rather than a long sequence of similarly weighted cards.

Above the fold:

```text
← Dwellings

Resident household 1
Brīvības iela 118

€0.00 outstanding    Email ✓    2 meters


[Overview] [Account] [Meters] [Residents] [History]
```

**Quick actions belong in the page header above the fold**, as you previously identified. They should include the handful of tasks an administrator actually performs:

```text
Enter readings
Create / review invoice
Add adjustment
Message resident
⋯
```

Do not make the user scroll through account activity and forms before reaching actions.

Below that, use sections or tabs instead of a continuous 1,500px-long configuration page.

Suggested structure:

```text

   Billing details
   Delivery preferences
   Resident access

   Balance
   Ledger
   Adjustments

Meters
   Active meters

   Periods
```

### Modals

Keep modals rare.

Use them for:

- irreversible confirmation;
- destructive action;
- explicit workflow transition.

Do not use Art Nouveau flourishes around dialog frames. The “Riga-ness” should come from color, proportion, typography, and linework.

### Decorative filigree

A single reusable ornament could provide brand distinction.

For example, create a thin vector motif derived from a simplified Art Nouveau curve:

```text
──────╮   ╭──────
      ╰───╯
```

or a vertical version.

Allow it only for:

- major marketing headings;
- document covers;
- annual reports;
- authentication page;
- important empty states.

Never use it between every form field.

That concentration of ornament is more faithful to the idea of elaborate architectural focal points than indiscriminate decoration. citeturn8search8

## Iconography, motion and implementation rules

### Icon system

Use a custom-feeling but operational line system:

```text
Canvas:            20 × 20 default
Large:             24 × 24
Stroke:            1.75px
Caps:              round
Joins:             round
Terminal radius:   subtle
Optical padding:   2px
```

The geometry should combine:

**Gothic structure**
→ straight verticals and clear axes

with:

**Jugendstil finish**
→ occasional curved terminals and asymmetric secondary strokes

and:

**modern UI discipline**
→ immediate recognition at 16–20px.

Create bespoke icons for product-specific concepts where generic icon libraries are weak:

```text
Dwelling
Period
Meter
Water reading
Invoice preparation
Invoice sent
Account balance
Payment reconciliation
Ledger adjustment
Building
Resident
```

Avoid mini illustrations masquerading as icons.

### Architectural motif library

A small vector library can unify brand and product.

Use four fundamental motifs:

**Arch**

```text
╭────╮
│    │
```

Art Nouveau / portals.

**Gable**

```text
 /\
/  \
```

National Romantic / medieval skyline.

**Panel**

```text
│  │  │  │
```

Wooden façades.

**Course**

```text
──────────
──────────
```

Masonry / limestone.

These should influence geometry, not become literal decorative clip-art.

### Motion philosophy: weight, not bounce

Architectural motion should feel like something with mass.

Avoid:

- elastic springs;
- exaggerated scale;
- overshoot;
- card wobble;
- parallax;
- decorative looping animations.

Use:

```text
Hover / press        100–140ms
Tooltip              120ms
Dropdown             160ms
Accordion             180–220ms
Drawer                220–260ms
Modal                 180–220ms
Page-content reveal   ≤280ms
```

Recommended entry easing:

```css
cubic-bezier(0.20, 0.80, 0.20, 1)
```

Exit:

```css
cubic-bezier(0.40, 0, 1, 1)
```

The conceptual behavior is:

> **settle into place rather than spring into place.**

Motion triggered by interaction should also respect user reduced-motion preferences; W3C specifically documents `prefers-reduced-motion` as the mechanism for reducing potentially problematic animation. citeturn7search0turn7search2

For reduced motion:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *,
  *::before,
  *::after {
    scroll-behavior: auto !important;
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
  }
}
```

Charts should continue the architecture rather than suddenly becoming dashboard-neon.

Suggested sequence:

```text
Amber      #C17B26
Brick      #9A493A
```

Use direct labels wherever possible.

Grid lines:

```text
limestone / stone-joint

```

For cold/hot water, retain intuitive differentiation, but mute it into the Riga palette rather than using saturated cyan/orange.

### The final visual character

The target is **not**:

> “an Art Nouveau accounting app.”

It is:

> **a contemporary Baltic civic product whose materials, proportions, typography, and fine details feel unmistakably at home in Riga.**

From a distance the interface should read as modern, calm, and highly functional.

At medium range, users should notice warmer limestone surfaces, Daugava blue, verdigris, disciplined vertical rhythm, and unusually thoughtful typography.

Only at close range should they discover the Riga-specific details: a curved icon terminal, a National Romantic display letter, a façade-like divider, a copper-patina accent, or a deliberately crafted ornament.

That hierarchy is what prevents the identity from becoming theme-park historicism while still making it substantially more ownable than the generic blue-and-grey SaaS visual language your current interface uses. Riga's actual built identity supports exactly this layered approach: UNESCO recognizes medieval fabric, Art Nouveau, and wooden architecture together, while the city's contemporary cultural architecture shows that local symbolism can coexist with modern function. citeturn1search0turn4view3turn2search0
