# Fix the dashboard's money and next-step gaps

The Namkopa dashboard today is a competent but passive status page: it shows three money cards, a list of dwellings that are missing data or overdue, seven status counts and a water total, and it never tells the admin where the month stands or what to do next once initial setup is finished. The strongest problem is the money block: "Total invoiced" is the sum of current charges only, while "Paid" and "Outstanding" are measured against the full amount due (current charges plus carried debt, credits and late fees), so for any dwelling with arrears the three cards do not add up, even though the code already computes the missing figures and does not display them (verified in `src/domain/periods/dashboard.ts`). The second problem is that the only "what next" logic is the first-time setup banner, which disappears permanently after the first invoice is sent; the header button reads "Open workbench" in every lifecycle state. For a Latvian house-coop admin of about 50 who thinks in Excel, the best-supported design is a short page of about four blocks: a plain-language month status with one next action, a progress line through the four steps (readings, invoices, sent, paid), a reconciled money summary, and a short exceptions list with amounts and due dates. Everything else (full status breakdown, debtor names, ledger, audit, water history) belongs one click away. The Latvian-law and Excel-practice evidence for this persona is thin: the legal cycle is sourced from secondary portals, the UI numbers come from WCAG and general older-user research, and the persona itself, the priority matrix and most Latvian wording are inference, not user-tested fact. Section 6 separates what is verified from what is inferred, and section 5 gives twelve ordered changes with effort and impact.

## What the dashboard and its documentation say today

The dashboard page is `src/pages/admin/o/[orgId]/dashboard/index.astro`, and everything below was verified by reading the working tree (28 files are modified or untracked against HEAD 943a2eb, and the dashboard file itself has about 275 changed lines that are not yet committed or logged). It renders in this order: a setup banner while onboarding is unfinished; a period header with a month heading, an OPEN or LOCKED badge and one primary button; a previous/next period navigation; three KPI cards (Total invoiced, Paid, Outstanding); a "Needs attention" list; a "Case status" panel with seven always-visible counts; and a "Consumption" card with cold and hot water in m³. The attention list contains only dwellings in MISSING_DATA or OVERDUE state, with no cap. A MISSING_DATA row offers "Add readings", which opens an in-page drawer. An OVERDUE row carries only a static sentence ("Payment is past its due date") and a link to the unfiltered Payments page, with no amount, due date or days late, although the amount is already fetched by the case query.

Lifecycle behaviour is where the page is weakest. Reading the code state by state, a brand-new organization sees the setup banner and an otherwise blank page. Once readings are complete, cases flip to READY, but the attention list says "Nothing needs attention right now" and only a buried count in the status panel hints that invoices can be generated. Draft invoices are already counted in "Total invoiced" because the query has no sent filter. Reversed or bounced payments reduce "Paid" silently. A locked newest period shows a disabled "Next period" and no way to create the following month from the dashboard. Any database error, including a bad `periodId`, silently redirects to the periods list with no error state. Unmatched bank transactions, invoice delivery failures and unread messages (other than a sidebar badge) are absent, even though the in-app admin guide tells the admin to use the dashboard for "unmatched payments, and delivery failures." The UI locale also defaults to English unless a cookie or the browser's `Accept-Language` says otherwise, although the spec names Latvian as the canonical language.

The documentation is thin and partly stale. The only normative dashboard requirement is a seven-bullet list in the spec (`ORCA_PROPERTY_BILLING_ASTRO_SPEC.md:2217-2228`), which the implementation matches exactly; the service blueprint adds that the dashboard "supports monitoring and prioritization" and explicitly says other service-quality signals are "not new dashboard requirements" (`SERVICE_DESIGN_BLUEPRINT.md:99, :150`). No persona document exists; the "Latvian co-op admin, about 50, Excel-minded" profile in this task is not written down anywhere in the repo, and the closest proxies are the spec's phrase "housing associations, cooperatives and small property managers" and the Excel/Windows-1257 migration context in ADR 0013. Stale or contradictory items to fix are `USER_JOURNEYS.md` lines 18 and 24 (it implies the dashboard flags "Ready to bill" and opens the drawer from the workbench; neither is true), `KNOWN_GAPS.md:189` (says readings are entered only in the workbench, which the dashboard drawer contradicts), the guide screenshot `public/guide/dashboard.jpg` (dated 2026-09-15, shows the old "Property Billing" brand and demo values around 70 million euro), and ADR 0012 item 9, which does not describe the new "Next step" and per-step links. The competitor reference (`EASYBILLY_REVIEW_2026-10-05.md`) records that Easybilly's Home has a monthly navigation, a needs-action area, building readiness, recent activity and a collection summary, and warns that setup "overclaims readiness"; Namkopa's banner has the same weakness, since it reports only configuration-complete, not billing-ready or ready-to-send.

## The admin's month, and what the page must show

The legal and practical cycle for a Latvian house association is better documented than its Excel practice. Owners are meant to read meters every month and pass readings to whoever calculates the payable share (MK Nr. 524, point 2) ([Vēstnesis](https://www.vestnesis.lv/ta/id/276739)). The manager keeps the bank account, pays suppliers, prepares the annual budget and financial accounting, and reports in writing at least once a year on funds used ([LV portāls guide 2026](https://lvportals.lv/skaidrojumi/393279-celvedis-daudzdzivoklu-majas-parvaldisana-2026); [LV portāls 36503](https://lvportals.lv/e-konsultacijas/36503-tiesibas-sanemt-no-parvaldnieka-informaciju-par-lidzeklu-izlietosanu-2025)). There is no single splitting formula: management costs default to ownership share, while water, waste and heat can be split by meter, area, residents or apartment count depending on regulation, contract and owner decisions ([LV portāls 38670](https://lvportals.lv/e-konsultacijas/38670-normativajos-aktos-dzivojamas-majas-izdevumiem-nav-noteikta-viena-aprekina-formula-2026)). Owners may demand the basis of a charge, including supplier invoices ([LV portāls 38334](https://lvportals.lv/e-konsultacijas/38334-dzivokla-ipasnieka-tiesibas-iepazities-ar-informaciju-par-aprekinatajiem-maksajumiem-2026)). Two rule-based alerts are documented: where readings are late, consumption is estimated from the dwelling's three-month average for no more than three months ([LV portāls 30168](https://lvportals.lv/e-konsultacijas/30168-ja-nenodod-udens-skaititaja-radijumus-2023)), and a house-meter-versus-apartment-meters gap above 20% for three consecutive months triggers a duty to investigate and notify owners within two months (MK 524, point 14) ([Vēstnesis](https://www.vestnesis.lv/ta/id/276739)). Several of these sources are lvportals e-consultations that state they are informational, and the primary texts on likumi.lv could not be fetched, so statute detail is secondary. Real pain points are documented mainly through the law and press (water differences from late readings, disputes over heat splitting, owner demands for justification, uncertainty over who answers for a neighbour's debt ([LV portāls 38418](https://lvportals.lv/e-konsultacijas/38418-dzivoklu-ipasnieku-kopibai-nav-solidaras-atbildibas-par-citu-ipasnieku-paradiem-2026))); no first-person testimony from small-association administrators or Excel templates was found.

From that cycle, the admin's questions on opening the tool follow the month in order: where are we, who has not given readings, can I bill, did the bills go out, who has paid and who owes, does anything look wrong, and what is coming. This question list and the priority matrix below are inference, anchored to the repo's own journey ("What needs attention now?") and to the legal cycle, not to observed users.

Two content gaps need product decisions, not UI work. The monthly cycle includes comparing the sum billed with supplier invoices and paying suppliers, but Namkopa has no supplier-cost, budget or reserve-fund model (the Easybilly review confirms budgets and documents are deferred), so "does my total match the supplier" cannot be shown. The annual owner report and kopsapulce deadlines are likewise outside the product today.

## How to show it: size, status, structure and Latvian wording

The evidence for an older, low-confidence user is firmest where it comes from WCAG, not from age-specific research. Text must reach 4.5:1 contrast at AA and 7:1 at AAA ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)); pointer targets must be at least 24 px at AA and 44 px at AAA ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [W3C 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)); and colour may not be the only carrier of meaning ([W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)). For type size, Bernard, Liao and Mills found 14 pt (about 18.7 px) more legible and preferred over 12 pt among older adults ([ACM abstract mirror](https://www.springerprofessional.de/doi/10.1145/634067.634173)), while a 2008 eye-tracking study reported that sizes above 14 pt slowed reading sweeps (secondary report), so the evidence is moderate and mixed. GOV.UK uses 19 px body text ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/font-override-classes/)). A defensible baseline is 16 px as the floor and 18 px for body text, line height 1.5, primary controls at 44 px, 7:1 for body text and key figures. NN/g reports older users make more mistakes and recommends tolerant input formats and fix-oriented error messages ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/)).

Verified against the repo, contrast is not the problem: the light-mode muted text is 5.4 to 5.8:1 and the badge tones are 4.9 to 7.8:1, all passing AA. The problem is size: operational text is mostly 12 to 14 px, the main button is 42 px with a 14 px label, and only the period links, attention actions and status rows reach 44 px. Statuses already combine icon, text and colour, which is correct. What needs fixing is tone semantics: READY and OVERDUE share the amber warning tone, and "Outstanding" is amber even when nothing is due, which signals a problem where there may be none.

For structure, NN/g advises a dashboard focused on one task and glanceable on one screen, with at most two levels of progressive disclosure ([NN/g dashboards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/); [NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)), and recommends position- and length-based charts (bars, bullet charts) over pies, donuts and gauges. Hidden navigation reduced desktop discoverability by over 20% and slowed tasks by at least 39% in a 179-participant study, though some effects were marginal ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)); the desktop sidebar is already visible, which is right. The "about four blocks above the fold" figure is an inference from Cowan's estimate of roughly four working-memory chunks ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/)), not a published dashboard rule, and the popular "7 plus or minus 2" should not be cited as a UI rule. Recommended order, all inferred from these principles: month heading with a one-sentence status; one primary next-action button; a four-step progress line with text states ("Pabeigts", "Jāizdara", "Vēl nav pieejams"); the money summary as a small table or three cards with a stated basis; an exceptions list capped at about five rows with "show all N"; the rest behind links. Tables remain the right form for comparable numeric rows (Excel familiarity), with a visible totals row, zeros shown as "0,00 €" rather than blank, and tabular numerals. Avoid tooltips as the only explanation, auto-refreshing numbers, and toasts for errors ([NN/g validations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/)).

Latvian formatting is partly verified. Running `Intl` with `lv-LV` on Node 22 (CLDR 48) yields "1 234 567,89 €", short dates like "5.10.2026.", and "2026. g. oktobris" for month and year; CLDR also gives nominative month names only ([CLDR lv.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/unicode-org/cldr/main/common/main/lv.xml)). Four-digit amounts are not grouped by default ("1234,50 €"), which can misalign columns, so money columns should force grouping. A 2014 Latvian Language Commission decision, reported secondhand, places the currency after the amount with a non-breaking space and prefers "eiro" in running text ([LV portāls](https://lvportals.lv/skaidrojumi/260812-eiro-un-cents-latviesu-valoda-2014)). Sentences such as "in October" need the locative "oktobrī", which the library does not produce; this is from general Latvian grammar, not a retrieved source, and it argues for nominative constructions ("Periods: 2026. gada oktobris") or hand-written month tables. Address form (Jūs versus neutral phrasing) has no authority source; neutral imperatives and nouns ("Pārbaudiet summas", "Rēķini ir gatavi") are a safe default. Existing wording inconsistencies are verified: the dashboard says `Rēķinu kopsumma` while the workbench says `Izrakstīts` for the same figure, the setup counter renders "1 / 5 pabeigts" by lowercasing a status word, and Latvian help text in `i18n.ts` (around lines 1486 and 1495) embeds English enum codes such as DRAFT and PREPARED.

## Where Codex agreed, where it overreached, and what it got wrong

Codex (CLI v0.164.0, `gpt-5.6-sol`, read-only) was run in two passes, content and UI. The first run is discarded: the sandbox could not spawn a shell, so Codex fell back to the GitHub connector, which sees only committed HEAD, and its line numbers were stale. The second run embedded the working-tree sources with line numbers, but Codex still saw only fragments of `global.css` (lines 1-300, 395-430, 500-560, 630-810) and `i18n.ts` (370-430), which explains two of its errors. Every claim below was checked against the working tree; 28 of 32 checked items held up.

Agreement is strong on eight points, all verified in code. The financial block is inconsistent (Total invoiced is `currentCharges` only; Paid and Outstanding use `amountDue`; the draft/prepared invoices are included; "Paid" is allocations against this period's invoices, not cash received this month). The dashboard shows what is wrong but not progress, and has no post-setup next step. Overdue rows lack amount and due date. `readingDeadline` and `invoiceDueDate` exist in the schema but are never shown. Water totals are a partial sum of submitted m³ readings with no completeness or comparison. Wording and 12-14 px sizes suit this persona poorly. READY and OVERDUE share the amber tone. Dead CSS selectors (`.dashboard-totals`, `.dashboard-period-list`) mean the KPI icon colour rules never apply, and each KPI card renders an `<h2>`. Codex's other verified points include that the drawer refresh updates only the attention and status sections, not the KPIs, and that the dashboard and workbench can disagree after an org currency change (low practical impact).

Three Codex claims were verified wrong and should not be acted on. First, that custom links lack a `:focus-visible` treatment: a global rule (`outline: 3px solid var(--color-primary-hover)`, `global.css:320-323`) exists, and Codex had not seen that part of the file. Second, that "No billing rule applies" and "no active meter" may lack Latvian translations: both are present (`i18n.ts:994-995`, and in Russian). Third, that `space-y-6` plus the page-header bottom margin doubles the gap above the period navigation: this is a normal flow layout where adjacent block margins collapse, so it is probably wrong, though no browser check was done. One Codex citation was also misapplied: `USER_JOURNEYS.md:124`, offered for "oldest due date, largest debtors", is the resident-portal row; "largest or oldest debtors" is Codex's idea, not a documented requirement.

The disagreements are about scope and order, not facts. Codex puts collection rate, partial payments, largest and oldest debtors, delivery-failure counts, unmatched payments, overpayments and consumption variance in its must-see and should-see buckets; that is a mature-product dashboard, and the service blueprint explicitly limits the dashboard to monitoring and prioritization. For a small-association admin who wants a month summary, most of this belongs one click away. On hierarchy, Codex keeps the money cards first and attention second; the opposing view, shared by this report, is that a progress-plus-next-action band should sit above or beside the money cards because the page's single generic button gives no direction. This is a genuine opinion split with no user evidence either way, and it is a good candidate for a quick comparison with two or three real admins. Codex also missed several things: `getCurrentOpenPeriod` does not exclude the starting-readings period, so the dashboard can select a period the navigation does not list (read from code, not run); the attention count and list come from two separate queries; the allocation and reversal queries load every allocation in the organization on every page view; English enum codes appear inside Latvian help text; and "Īpašums" versus "Dzīvoklis". Codex's Latvian suggestions are mostly sound ("Vēl neapmaksāts", "Atvērt mēneša norēķinus", "Var izrakstīt rēķinu"), but neither Codex nor this report is a native-speaker review.

## What is verified, what is inferred

Verified by reading or running code in this repository: everything in the "current state" section, including the block list, the KPI arithmetic, lifecycle behaviour, missing data (carried balances, deadlines, unmatched payments), the documentation inconsistencies listed, the Latvian strings and their line numbers, the contrast ratios (recomputed from the design tokens), the dead CSS, and 28 of 32 Codex claims. Verified externally from retrieved pages: the WCAG success criteria, the NN/g findings on dashboards, progressive disclosure, hamburger menus and senior users, Cowan's chunk estimate, CLDR Latvian month names and `Intl` output, and the secondary-portal statements of Latvian law cited above (secondary, because likumi.lv was unreachable and MK 524 was read only in part).

Inferred, and not to be presented as fact in product docs: the persona (age, skill, volunteer status, building size, Excel habits), the reconstructed Excel structure, the admin's question order, the entire priority matrix and its frequency and cost ratings, the "about four blocks" limit, 16-18 px as the ideal size for this user, every Latvian wording proposal and the Jūs-versus-neutral address choice, the locative-month-name rule, the GDPR-driven default of hiding debtor names, and the effort and impact ratings. Not done at all: no live render, keyboard, zoom or dark-mode review of the dashboard; no user research; no native Latvian review; no statute-text verification; no fixed legal deadlines for readings or billing were found, since the sources say owners or contracts decide. Law is also in motion: a draft replacement for the management law was still in inter-institutional review in mid-2026 (secondary summary, adoption unconfirmed), and amendments to the apartment-property law took effect on 29 May 2026 ([iFinanses](https://ifinanses.lv/bizness/raksti/uznemuma-vadiba/uznemuma-vadiba/biz-new-jauni-instrumenti-un-skaidraki-noteikumi-maju-parvaldisana/31115)).

For the follow-up doc update, the concrete targets are: add a persona note (flagged as assumed) to the product docs; replace the spec's seven-bullet dashboard list with the final block list and priority matrix; correct `USER_JOURNEYS.md` lines 18 and 24 and add a setup state; remove or fix `KNOWN_GAPS.md:189`; extend ADR 0012 item 9 for the new banner and add a short ADR for the dashboard's money definitions; regenerate `public/guide/dashboard.jpg` and revise the guide step that promises unmatched payments and delivery failures; and log the uncommitted dashboard work in `WORK_LOG.md`.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the problem. The dashboard does not mainly lack features; it lacks a point of view. Its raw material (amount due, carried debt, late fees, deadlines, case counts, overdue amounts) is largely computed already, and the biggest improvement is to say, in plain Latvian, which of four steps the month is in, what the next one is, and how the three money figures relate. That is a labelling-and-composition task, cheap compared with the analytics Codex proposed, and it fits both the product's own "monitoring and prioritization" principle and the Excel-trained admin's wish to see a column that adds up.

The weakest link is the evidence base, not the design. Latvian authority guidance on date and address style is absent, there is no testimony from the real persona, and the one live disagreement (money first versus next-action first) cannot be settled from desk research. The most valuable next step is not more building but a short session with two or three actual coop administrators on a populated screenshot and a native Latvian read of the new copy, followed by updating the product docs with the persona and the verified/inferred distinction kept explicit.
